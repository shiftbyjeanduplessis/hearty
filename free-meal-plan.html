Nutrition basics

What a good GLP-1 meal plan should do

GLP-1 medication can reduce appetite and slow digestion, so the meal plan has to protect nutrition while keeping meals small, familiar and easy to repeat.

  • Protein at every meal. Prioritise lean protein first so smaller meals still support muscle retention while weight is dropping.
  • Do not skip nutrition when appetite is low. A light yoghurt bowl, eggs, tuna, chicken, beans or a protein drink is better than an empty day.
  • Build plates in order. Start with protein, add vegetables or fruit, then add a sensible starch if it helps energy and satisfaction.
  • Use slow, useful carbohydrates. Oats, potatoes, rice, whole wheat wraps, beans and fruit can support energy without turning the plan into a sugar-heavy diet.
  • Keep fibre steady. Vegetables, fruit, oats, legumes and whole grains help gut regularity, especially when food volume is lower.
  • Increase fibre with fluids. More fibre without enough liquid can make constipation worse, so hydration has to rise with it.
  • Plan hydration deliberately. Smaller appetite often means less drinking too, so the plan should keep water, soups or water-rich foods visible.
  • Manage nausea with meal size. Smaller, plainer meals are often easier than large, rich or greasy meals on rough days.
  • Protect micronutrients. Fruit, vegetables, dairy or fortified alternatives, legumes and varied proteins help cover vitamins and minerals when portions shrink.
  • Include calcium and vitamin D sources. Yoghurt, milk, fortified alternatives, tinned fish with bones or appropriate supplements may matter when intake is low.
  • Keep healthy fats measured. Avocado, olive oil, nuts and seeds are useful, but large high-fat meals can feel heavy during delayed digestion.
  • Use simple snacks strategically. Protein yoghurt, cottage cheese, boiled eggs, tuna, fruit or a small handful of nuts can close nutrition gaps.
  • Avoid extreme restriction. The goal is consistent nourishment, not starving, skipping meals or cutting out whole food groups unnecessarily.
  • Match food to injection rhythm. Some people need gentler meals around injection days and more complete meals when appetite improves.
  • Make the plan repeatable. Familiar foods and realistic portions beat complicated recipes that disappear after one motivated week.
Important note: Hearty is a GLP-1 companion app and does not replace medical care. This free meal plan is general education and lifestyle support only. Please follow your healthcare provider’s advice, especially if you have medical conditions or prescribed dietary instructions.
